About

Most of what I build starts as a mechanical problem and ends up needing a circuit — a frame that has to rotate, a tile that has to generate its own power, a claw that has to grab something exactly right. I like living in that overlap.

My mechanical engineering coursework gives me the structure and manufacturing side; the electrical engineering minor I'm adding this spring fills in the sensing, wiring, and control half of that same problem.

That mix started in high school through three years of competitive engineering design with the Technology Student Association — building an obstacle-course UAV, a rubber-band-powered aircraft, and a multi-function picture frame — and it's carried into college through a campus energy-awareness project, a piezoelectric aircraft research paper, and my current work on the Aerostructures subteam of Pitt's SOAR project team.

Featured projects

Three builds that span the mechanical-to-electrical range, from a hand-sketched energy-harvesting concept to laser-cut mechanisms.

TENG Campus Energy Tile

First-Year Engineering · Fall 2025

Pitt's clean energy infrastructure is invisible to most students — this project asked how to make it visible and interactive instead. Researched and designed a triboelectric nanogenerator (TENG) tile that converts footstep pressure into electricity, built a physical mockup of the layer stack, then wired a working circuit that lights an LED on contact. Presented the concept campus-wide with event flyers, turning an invisible utility into something people could see and feel.

Mechanical Design Circuit Wiring Sustainability Research Public Engagement

Multi-Function Rotating Picture Frame

TSA Manufacturing Prototype · Senior Year

A photo frame that only displays photos felt like a wasted build, so this one also works as a whiteboard, a set of storage drawers, and a pencil holder — with a laser-engraved perpetual calendar built into its rotating face. Disassembled and rewired a small speaker into the housing, 3D-printed a rotating base mount, and finished the piece with a painted finish. The result: a compact, lightweight frame that rotates cleanly while packing in all four functions.

Interactive Adventure Storybook

TSA Children's Story · Placed high at states + interview round

A picture book where reading is only half the activity: each page had to unlock a real-world clue, experiment, or activity by the end of the book. Wrote the story, illustrated it digitally, then printed and hand-bound the physical copy — taking it from blank concept to a finished object a judge could actually hold and read.

Obstacle-Course UAV

TSA UAV · Sophomore Year

Team-built a drone to navigate an obstacle course, then locate and lift ground targets with a 3D-printed, servo-actuated claw. Wired the servo to the controller and tuned flight stability through the flight-configuration software. Result: stable, controlled flight with a working claw.

Rubber-Band Flight Endurance

TSA Flight Endurance · Senior Year

Built and trimmed a rubber-band-powered fixed-wing aircraft from a kit, then ran repeated flight tests indoors — logging times and adjusting trim after each run. Reached 60–90 seconds of sustained flight through iteration alone.
